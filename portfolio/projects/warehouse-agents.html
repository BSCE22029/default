What's real here
  • Auction (contract-net): when orders are waiting, every idle robot bids its real shortest-path cost (BFS to the pickup, then to the drop-off). The lowest bid across all robot and order pairs wins. The log shows the actual bids.
  • Cooperative space-time A*: robots plan one after another. Each planned route is written into a reservation table (cell, time) and (swap, time). Later robots search a 3D space (x, y, time) that includes waiting, so they step aside or wait instead of colliding. Idle robots park and count as obstacles.
  • Collisions are counted as two robots in one cell, or two robots swapping cells in one tick. With reservations the count is zero or close to it (in my test runs, 0 to 3 per 800 ticks, against 90 to 300 without). It is not guaranteed zero: planning is greedy and cooperative, not optimal, and it does not detect deadlock. Without collision handling robots look faster only because they drive straight through each other.
  • Nothing is scripted: the benchmark reruns the full simulation for each strategy from the same random seed. Results vary with robot count and arrival rate, so try a few settings.
